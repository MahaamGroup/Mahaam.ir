# Mahaam — Illustration System

## Purpose

Illustration should make abstract concepts—orientation, comparison, routes, conditions, and connection—more understandable. It must not become decorative corporate artwork.

## Preferred approaches

- Abstract layered geometry with depth and soft light
- Fine-line topographic or route systems
- Modular diagrams with a refined editorial quality
- Architectural, map-like, or spatial compositions
- Restrained 3D forms with matte material and low-saturation color

## Placement

Use illustration primarily in the hero, journey explorer, assessment teaser, and selected empty states. One strong illustration per section is enough.

## Composition rules

- Maintain generous negative space around copy.
- Use palette tokens only.
- Keep forms tactile but not glossy, metallic, or toy-like.
- Avoid literal flags, maps, planes, passports, document stamps, and globes.
- Do not use cartoon characters or generic “diverse team” vectors.

## Diagram language

When explaining the Mahaam process, diagrams should be readable at a glance and have a human tone. Show relationships, sequence, or convergence—never an opaque scoring formula.

## Placeholder instruction

If no custom illustration exists, create a minimal abstract placeholder built from the approved motifs rather than inserting unrelated stock art.
