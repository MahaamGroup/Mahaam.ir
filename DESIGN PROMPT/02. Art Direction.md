# Mahaam — Art Direction

## Central image

Imagine an editorial atlas for consequential decisions: deep, calm fields of color; soft light across layers; precise information surfaces emerging from an atmospheric background. The experience has depth, but never visual noise.

## Composition

- Favor confident asymmetry inside a disciplined grid.
- Place dense information against generous breathing room.
- Use large, cropped visual forms and controlled overlap to create tension.
- Let one dominant visual idea lead each section; do not decorate every gap.
- Treat cards as architectural surfaces, not floating stickers.

## Materials

Use a restrained combination of:

- Matte off-white and cool gray paper-like surfaces
- Ink-like navy and midnight backgrounds
- Translucent layered panels only where hierarchy benefits
- Fine grain, hairline grids, contour lines, and soft blurred light
- Carefully cropped documentary photography or abstract 3D-like geometry

## Light and depth

Depth should come from tonal contrast, overlap, ambient shadows, and light—not from heavy drop shadows. Dark sections may use a subtle luminous core behind key content. Light sections may carry a faint grid, grain, or gradient field.

## Section rhythm

Alternate immersion and relief:

1. Immersive, dark hero
2. Light recognition section
3. Toned process / explanation section
4. Rich route explorer
5. Calm assessment section
6. Dark closing invitation

Do not alternate colors mechanically. Each transition must support the story.

## Photography art direction

When photography is needed, use documentary-feeling, editorial, naturally lit scenes: purposeful people at work, contemporary architecture, research, workshops, or international professional settings. People should look engaged in meaningful work—not posed for a brochure.

## Scale

Use large headlines, generous margins, and bold visual crops. Premium does not mean oversized everything; it means deliberate proportion.

## Placeholder asset direction

Use labeled placeholders that are easy to replace later, such as `MAHAAM / IMAGE 01 / PROFESSIONAL CONTEXT`. Never rely on a visual that contains essential text.
